Methodology

"Quantum PCA" is implemented as a trainable quantum autoencoder: a parameterized circuit U(θ) (Qiskit's RealAmplitudes) is trained so that, after encoding a data sample as a quantum state, a chosen subset of "trash" qubits collapses toward |0… 0⟩. Because U is unitary, discarding those trash qubits and applying U(θ)† reconstructs an approximation of the original state — the quantum analogue of PCA's encode → truncate → decode pipeline. Reconstruction quality is measured with a SWAP test against a fresh copy of the true input state.

Classical PCA (scikit-learn) is run on the identical, reduced-and-normalized feature matrix, with n_components chosen to match the quantum method's latent-register capacity, for a like-for-like comparison.

Limitations & proposed improvements

  • Zero-padding cost. Wine at 6 qubits is 51/64 zero-padded, so those results mostly measure how the autoencoder handles empty amplitude space rather than real feature reduction. Improvement: PCA-informed feature selection, or block-encoding two samples per register instead of padding.
  • State-preparation overhead. initialize() costs gates that grow with vector length, so reported depth/gate-count partly reflects loading the data, not the autoencoder itself — a known limitation of amplitude-encoded quantum ML. Improvement: report encoder-only depth separately from total depth.
  • Mini-batch, gradient-free training. COBYLA optimizes a small fixed subsample and needs a large iteration budget to converge well. Improvement: a gradient-based optimizer (parameter-shift + Adam, or SPSA) typically reaches a better optimum.
  • Evaluation overhead. The SWAP test needs 2n+1 qubits, more than the encoder's own n qubits — a standard measurement-subroutine cost, reported separately from the encoder's qubit count.
  • Simplified noise model. Depolarizing noise approximates but doesn't capture coherent errors, crosstalk or non-Markovian effects. Improvement: import a real backend noise model via NoiseModel.from_backend(...) where IBM Quantum access is available.
